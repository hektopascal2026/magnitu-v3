I. What is Magnitu?

A personal relevance engine that learns what you care about.

Magnitu scores every incoming article, email, and legal notice on a scale from 0 to 100 based on your priorities. It watches your judgments—as you read entries and assign them one of four labels, Magnitu builds a model to predict your judgment on entries you haven't seen yet.

The Four Labels

Investigation Lead The highest-value category. Points to a story worth pursuing, evidence of wrongdoing, or connections between actors.
Important Relevant to your beat and worth reading, but not an actionable lead on its own.
Background Tangentially related or good to know, but not something you'd act on today.
Noise Irrelevant to your work, such as press releases, off-topic content, or duplicates.

II. Architecture & Workspaces

Designed for newsrooms where teams share a data pool but have diverging beats.

  • The Mothership: All profiles share a single, global connection to the main Seismo instance. You sync entries once, and embedding computation runs once per entry for all profiles.
  • Multi-Profile Architecture: One Magnitu instance can run multiple isolated profiles. Each profile trains its own model on its own labels and manages its own recipe.
  • Lightweight Satellites: Each profile can push its specific scores to a dedicated "satellite" Seismo installation (e.g., one PHP install per topic). The satellite maintains its own database for Magnitu scores and state, while the raw entries stream from the mothership.
  • Sharing (.magnitu files): Models can be exported as a zip archive containing the manifest.json, labels.json, model.joblib, calibration.json, and recipe.json.
  • Forking: You can "fork" a team's base model to inherit their shared baseline judgments (e.g., ignoring general press releases), which you can then customize for your specific beat.

III. Version History & Narrative Evolution

The structural transformation of Magnitu from a local sorting prototype into a distributed, multi-profile intelligence grid.

1. The Dawn of Magnitu: Version 1.x (The Prototype Era)

The project began as an experimental prototype designed to solve information overload for professional monitoring desks. In this initial stage, the platform focused on validating a basic premise: could a system learn an individual analyst's specific editorial priorities based entirely on user-driven relevance feedback?

Key Technical & Operational Milestones

  • The Foundational Scoring Scale: Established the core 0 to 100 relevance scale to rank incoming entries, shifting the workflow from a basic chronological feed to a priority-sorted stream.
  • The Four-Label Taxonomy: Defined the rigid four-class system to categorize intelligence assets: Investigation Lead, Important, Background, and Noise.
  • User-Driven Feedback Loop: Introduced a simple interface where an analyst could read through incoming documents, tag them, and build a local classification profile.
💡 What This Means for Laymen

In the prototype era, Magnitu was essentially a smart, digital sorting tray. Instead of forcing you to look at an endless, overwhelming wall of news sorted blindly by time, the system watched what you manually clicked on. If you kept flagging corporate financial filings as important and ignoring generic PR wires, it tried its best to push corporate text to the top of your feed.

Concrete Example: Imagine an analyst covering corporate malfeasance. An article arrives titled "Federal Prosecutor Opens Probe into Swiss Bank Entity." In v1.x, the analyst manually tags this as an Investigation Lead. The system logs this and looks for direct keyword matches in future articles. If another article arrives containing the words "Federal Prosecutor" or "Probe," its score gets bumped up slightly on the 0–100 dashboard slider.

2. The Cognitive Leap: Version 2.x (The Deep Learning & Distillation Bridge)

As data volumes expanded, simple keyword matching and basic statistical text filters hit a wall. They struggled intensely with linguistic nuances, metaphors, and cross-lingual alerts (e.g., matching a hidden Swiss market-access impact within a document written entirely in French or Italian). Version 2.x completely overhauled the system's core intelligence by introducing deep learning language processing.

Key Technical & Operational Milestones

  • Deep Transformers (xlm-roberta-base): Replaced superficial word-matching with a heavy, multilingual transformer model. This allowed Magnitu to map whole blocks of text into a shared mathematical vector space where conceptual meanings land near each other regardless of spelling or language.
  • The Distillation Bridge: Created a distinct architectural boundary. Because the primary production platform (Seismo) runs on high-speed, lightweight PHP, it cannot natively run heavy, resource-intensive Python deep learning frameworks. Version 2.x introduced Knowledge Distillation, using a simple TF-IDF "student" model to mimic the complex transformer "teacher's" predictions.
  • The Keyword Recipe Export: The student model's insights were compiled into a lightweight JSON file containing weighted keywords (unigrams, bigrams, and trigrams). Pushing this "recipe" to PHP achieved sub-millisecond scoring directly on the live server without needing specialized AI hardware in production.
💡 What This Means for Laymen

Magnitu upgraded from a basic keyword finder to a system that understands the meaning of sentences across multiple languages. Because this "big AI brain" was too heavy and slow to run every single millisecond a page loaded on your website, the system had the big brain act as a teacher. It read the data, figured out the deep patterns, and wrote a simplified, highly effective "cheat sheet" (the keyword recipe) for your web server to use instantly.

Concrete Example: A French EUR-Lex notice discusses accès au marché des pays tiers without mentioning Switzerland. You previously labeled similar English and German trade-impact text as Important. The transformer groups the concepts; the distilled recipe learns phrases like "third country" and "market access" so Seismo's PHP scorer still flags the French item high on your dashboard—even though the exact Swiss keyword never appears.

3. The Modern Automation & Multi-Profile Grid: Version 3.0 to 3.5 (The Satellite Era)

While Version 2.x established powerful modeling capabilities, it operated largely as a monolithic tool that struggled with cold-start data problems and manual tuning. The Version 3.x series—culminating in Version 3.5—was built for distributed newsrooms: one shared entry pool, many isolated editorial beats, and independent Satellite Seismo installs per profile.

[Mothership: Global Seismo Input]
       │
       ├──► Cached Multilingual E5 Embeddings (Computed Once)
       │
       ├──► [Profile A: Tax Beat] ──► LogReg Head ──► Soft Distillation ──► [Satellite PHP Install A]
       │
       └──► [Profile B: Trade Beat] ──► LogReg Head ──► Soft Distillation ──► [Satellite PHP Install B]

Key Technical & Operational Milestones

  • Multi-Profile & Satellite Architecture: Embeddings run once globally; each profile trains on its own labels and pushes scores to a dedicated Satellite PHP install.
  • Gemini Synthetic Scorer: Optional batch labeling with reasoning notes to accelerate convergence before manual review.
  • Multilingual E5 (multilingual-e5-base): Contrastive embeddings with passage: prefixing and longer legal-body context.
  • Natural Language Metadata: Publisher and feed context wrapped in fluent prose instead of raw source_type=… database strings.
  • Logistic Regression Heads: Regularized linear classifiers on frozen embeddings—stable with modest label counts while E5 carries semantic depth.
  • Out-of-Fold Temperature Scaling: Calibrated probabilities before push; sparse classes safely default to T=1.0.
  • Soft-Label Distillation: TF-IDF student trained on the teacher's full probability vectors, not just argmax labels.
  • Recipe Cap Optimization: Grid search over export caps on labeled holdout entries before the recipe ships to Seismo.
  • Label-Aware Time Decay: Optional training weights: noise fades faster; investigation_lead and important rows keep full weight by default.
💡 What This Means for Laymen

Version 3.x is built for a newsroom where everyone reads the same firehose but cares about different things. The heavy reading (embeddings) happens once. Your tax desk and your trade desk each get their own "brain" and their own Seismo dashboard, without duplicating the entire ingest pipeline.

Concrete Example: The mothership ingests the same EU implementing act. The Tax profile—trained on corporate tax precedents—scores it as low-priority Background. The Trade profile—trained on third-country market-access leads—flags the same document as an Investigation Lead at 0.89 and pushes that score only to its Satellite install, while Tax's satellite stays quiet.

IV. Workflow & Strategy

How you interact with Magnitu and guide its learning.

The Learning Loop

Magnitu improves through a repeating five-step cycle. Each cycle sharpens the model's accuracy.

1

Sync

Pull new entries from the main Seismo feed aggregator. Run a standard "Sync" for quick incremental updates or a "Full Sync" for source-by-source backfill and embedding completion.

2

Label

Read entries and assign them one of the four categories. Each label serves as a concrete training example.

3

Train

Magnitu builds a completely new model from all your historical labels. You need at least {{ config.min_labels_to_train or 20 }} labels to start.

4

Score & Push

The model scores every entry and sends those numbers, along with a lightweight keyword "recipe", to Seismo.

5

Review & Correct

Check the Top 30 page to review the model's highest-ranked entries. Reclassifying errors targets exact areas of model confusion and yields the highest-value training data.

Advanced Labeling Strategies

The Smart Queue

Magnitu mixes several strategies to surface entries that will improve the model fastest:

Uncertain

Entries where the model's probabilities are evenly spread.

Conflict

Entries where the deep local model and the lightweight PHP recipe disagree.

Diverse

Entries from underrepresented source categories to broaden the model's experience.

New

The most recent unlabeled entries.

Reasoning Notes & Legal Patterns

Reasoning Notes: The deep model does not read your annotations directly. Instead, explaining a label lets the recipe distiller extract phrase patterns you vouched for and boost them in the Seismo recipe. When enabled, a Reasoning-Weight Boost also gives those rows extra influence during training so your stated priorities are less likely to be drowned out by bulk noise elsewhere in the dataset.

Legislation Monitoring: For tracking foreign legislation (e.g., Swiss impacts from EU law), Magnitu learns to spot hidden impacts by recognizing patterns across languages:

  • Exclusion language: "EU or EEA", "Binnenmarkt"
  • Third-country provisions: "Drittstaaten", "equivalence"
  • Compliance triggers: "CE-Kennzeichnung", "Konformitätsbewertung"

Workflow: Use the "Legislation" source filter to label 50–100 legal items, adding reasoning notes to boost terms. Check the Learned Legal Patterns panel on the Model page to ensure correct signals are picked up.

V. Under the Hood

The mechanics of how Magnitu understands text, trains models, and calculates scores.

How Magnitu Reads Text

Language Model: Magnitu uses a pre-trained multilingual embedding model (default {{ config.transformer_model_name or 'intfloat/multilingual-e5-base' }}). It is contrastively trained for semantic similarity across 100+ languages, so terms like "Untersuchung" and "investigation" land near each other in vector space. Document text is prefixed with passage: at embed time, as required by the E5 family.

Meaning Vectors: Each article is converted into a 768-dimensional semantic fingerprint from its title (repeated for emphasis), description, and a body excerpt (up to {{ config.embedding_content_cap or 3000 }} characters). Structured context—publisher, feed type, category, and matched legal-signal phrases—is prepended as natural-language sentences rather than raw key=value tags, which tends to work better with the multilingual E5 model. Long documents also contribute extractive snippets from the full body when legal patterns match beyond the excerpt cap. Up to {{ config.embedding_max_tokens or 512 }} tokens are encoded per entry; embeddings are computed once at sync and cached.

Training & Distillation

The Classifier Head: Each train fits a regularized logistic regression classifier on your cached embeddings (balanced class weights, optional per-label sample_weight for time-decay and reasoning boost). This lightweight linear head stays stable with modest label counts while the E5 embeddings carry most of the semantic work—useful for overlapping topics such as a company name appearing in both regulatory news and routine press releases.

Knowledge Distillation: Because Seismo runs on PHP, it cannot execute Python transformer models. Magnitu trains a simpler TF-IDF student using soft-label distillation: the teacher's full class probabilities (not just the argmax label) guide the student across all scored entries. Keyword weights (unigrams, bigrams, trigrams) are then exported as a JSON recipe so Seismo can approximate the deep model's scores in milliseconds.

Relevance Scores & Measuring Quality

Every entry gets a relevance score from 0 to 1 (shown as a percentage). Class probabilities are calibrated with temperature scaling fit on out-of-fold predictions from the training fold, so scores are less overconfident before push. Note: If a profile's data pool lacks diverse representation (such as classes containing fewer than two total examples), the calibration system safely defaults to an inactive baseline (T=1.0) to prevent dataset distortion.

The final score is a weighted composite of calibrated probabilities:

  • Investigation Lead: Weight 1.0
  • Important: Weight 0.66
  • Background: Weight 0.33
  • Noise: Weight 0.0

Measuring Quality

Magnitu holds back ~10–20% of your labels as a stable test set (the same entries stay in test across retrains, keyed by entry id). It calculates Accuracy and an F1 Score, which is crucial because it penalizes models that inflate accuracy by simply calling everything "noise".

Known Biases & Limitations

Text Length Keyword weights in the recipe are normalized to compensate. Local transformer body excerpts are capped at {{ config.embedding_content_cap or 3000 }} characters ({{ config.embedding_max_tokens or 512 }} tokens at encode time).
Source Bias The recipe includes source weights (e.g. RSS toward noise). The deep model ignores source types unless they use distinctive language.
Class Imbalance Rare classes (like leads) use balanced class weights during training. Reasoning and time-decay knobs adjust per-label sample_weight instead of duplicating rows.
Recency Bias Unless Label Time-Decay is enabled, all labels hold equal weight regardless of age.

VI. Configuration

Settings are divided into global (shared) and per-profile knobs.

Profile Settings (Saved Per Profile)

Minimum Labels to Train Default 20. The safety gate before a model can generalize.
Recipe Top Keywords Default 200. Number of words/phrases pushed to the Seismo recipe.
Alert Threshold Default 0.75. The score above which Seismo highlights an entry as an alert.
Lead Discovery Blend Default 0, Max 0.25. Mixes extra investigation_lead probability into the final relevance score.
Label Time-Decay Default Off. When enabled, uses a label-aware split: investigation_lead and important rows keep full weight by default, noise decays faster, and background follows the configured half-life—so old junk patterns fade while high-value precedents stay in play.
Recipe Optimization On by default at distillation time. Grid-searches unigram, phrase, and source export caps against labeled holdout entries to pick values that best track the full model's relevance scores before the recipe ships to Seismo.
Reasoning-Weight Boost Default Off. Assigns a heavier training multiplier to labels with reasoning.

Global Settings (Shared Across All Profiles)

Legal-Signal Patterns: A user-configured list of regex/phrases. Matches are scanned on the full entry body, surfaced in natural-language context and extractive snippets in the embedded text, and boosted in the Seismo recipe. Changing this list invalidates all cached embeddings globally.
