I. What is Magnitu?

A personal relevance engine that learns what you care about.

Magnitu scores every incoming article, email, and legal notice on a scale from 0 to 100 based on your priorities. It watches your judgments—as you read entries and assign them one of four labels, Magnitu builds a model to predict your judgment on entries you haven't seen yet.

The Four Labels

Investigation Lead The highest-value category. Points to a story worth pursuing, evidence of wrongdoing, or connections between actors.
Important Relevant to your beat and worth reading, but not an actionable lead on its own.
Background Tangentially related or good to know, but not something you'd act on today.
Noise Irrelevant to your work, such as press releases, off-topic content, or duplicates.

II. Architecture & Workspaces

Designed for newsrooms where teams share a data pool but have diverging beats.

  • The Mothership: All profiles share a single, global connection to the main Seismo instance. You sync entries once, and embedding computation runs once per entry for all profiles.
  • Multi-Profile Architecture: One Magnitu instance can run multiple isolated profiles. Each profile trains its own model on its own labels and manages its own recipe.
  • Lightweight Satellites: Each profile can push its specific scores to a dedicated "satellite" Seismo installation (e.g., one PHP install per topic). The satellite maintains its own database for Magnitu scores and state, while the raw entries stream from the mothership.
  • Sharing (.magnitu files): Models can be exported as a zip archive containing the manifest.json, labels.json, model.joblib, calibration.json, and recipe.json.
  • Forking: You can "fork" a team's base model to inherit their shared baseline judgments (e.g., ignoring general press releases), which you can then customize for your specific beat.

III. Workflow & Strategy

How you interact with Magnitu and guide its learning.

The Learning Loop

Magnitu improves through a repeating five-step cycle. Each cycle sharpens the model's accuracy.

1

Sync

Pull new entries from the main Seismo feed aggregator. Run a standard "Sync" for quick incremental updates or a "Full Sync" for source-by-source backfill and embedding completion.

2

Label

Read entries and assign them one of the four categories. Each label serves as a concrete training example.

3

Train

Magnitu builds a new classifier from your confirmed labels (minimum {{ config.min_labels_to_train or 20 }}). Training runs as a background job with a progress bar — embedding missing rows, calibration folds, recipe distillation, and ETA while you keep working.

4

Score & Push

The model scores every entry and sends those numbers, along with a lightweight keyword "recipe", to Seismo.

5

Review & Correct

Check the Top 30 page to review the model's highest-ranked entries. Reclassifying errors targets exact areas of model confusion and yields the highest-value training data.

Advanced Labeling Strategies

The Smart Queue

Magnitu mixes several strategies to surface entries that will improve the model fastest:

Uncertain

Entries where the model's probabilities are evenly spread.

Conflict

Surfaces entries where the heavy Python MLP Neural Network (the Teacher) and the lightweight PHP TF-IDF Recipe (the Student) strongly disagree on the score. Labeling these targets pinpoints precisely where the distillation recipe is failing to approximate the deep model, rapidly forcing the student to learn your beat's complex edge cases.

Diverse

Entries from underrepresented source categories to broaden the model's experience.

New

The most recent unlabeled entries.

Reasoning Notes & Legal Patterns

Reasoning Notes: The deep model does not read your annotations directly. Instead, explaining a label allows the recipe distiller to extract user-vouched phrase patterns and apply a bounded Reasoning-Weight Boost. This mathematically anchors those specific keywords to positive correlation targets, preventing general dataset noise from accidentally driving down the score of terms you manually flagged as critical. When enabled, it also gives those rows extra influence during training so your stated priorities are less likely to be drowned out by bulk noise elsewhere.

Legislation Monitoring: For tracking foreign legislation (e.g., Swiss impacts from EU law), Magnitu learns to spot hidden impacts by recognizing patterns across languages:

  • Exclusion language: "EU or EEA", "Binnenmarkt"
  • Third-country provisions: "Drittstaaten", "equivalence"
  • Compliance triggers: "CE-Kennzeichnung", "Konformitätsbewertung"

Workflow: Use the "Legislation" source filter to label 50–100 legal items, adding reasoning notes to boost terms. Check the Learned Legal Patterns panel on the Model page to ensure correct signals are picked up.

IV. Under the Hood

The mechanics of how Magnitu understands text, trains models, and calculates scores.

How Magnitu Reads Text

Language Model: Magnitu uses a pre-trained multilingual embedding model (default {{ config.transformer_model_name or 'intfloat/multilingual-e5-base' }}). It is contrastively trained for semantic similarity across 100+ languages, so terms like "Untersuchung" and "investigation" land near each other in vector space. Document text is prefixed with passage: at embed time, as required by the E5 family.

Meaning Vectors: Each article becomes a 768-dimensional fingerprint from its title (repeated for emphasis), description, and body excerpt. Publisher and source context are woven in as natural-language sentences, not raw field dumps. Legal-pattern hits pull extractive snippets from the full body. Long text is split into several ~1800-character chunks, each encoded by E5 ({{ config.embedding_max_tokens or 512 }} tokens per chunk), then length-weighted mean-pooled into one vector — so statutory bodies beyond the first window still matter. Embeddings are computed at sync and cached; run Full Sync after upgrades to refresh long lex items.

Training & Distillation

The Classifier Head: A regularized logistic regression head on frozen E5 embeddings (balanced class weights, optional per-label sample weights from time-decay and reasoning boost). Stable entry-id holdout (~10–20% test) keeps metrics comparable across retrains.

Synthetic vs human labels: Confirmed Gemini labels train at {{ config.synthetic_label_weight or 0.5 }}× the weight of human labels by default (synthetic_label_weight in profile training settings; set 1.0 to disable). Pending Gemini batch rows are excluded from recipe reasoning boost until you Accept.

Knowledge Distillation: Seismo runs keyword recipes in PHP, not neural nets. Magnitu trains a TF-IDF + LogReg student on the transformer's soft class probabilities across all entries, with human labels overriding at 3× weight. Keyword coefficients (unigrams through trigrams, plus source-type features) become the recipe. Export tuning uses a scorer that mirrors Seismo's PHP rules (once per keyword, synopsis-only for lex/Leg, accents preserved).

Relevance Scores & Measuring Quality

Every entry gets a relevance score from 0 to 1. Class probabilities use temperature scaling fit on out-of-fold logits (T=1.0 when classes are too sparse for OOF). Locally, the score is a weighted composite of those probabilities:

  • Investigation Lead: Weight 1.0
  • Important: Weight 0.80
  • Background: Weight 0.20
  • Noise: Weight 0.0

On Push to Seismo, scores in that batch are converted to percentile ranks (0–1, ties averaged) before upload. Ordering is unchanged, but the spread avoids everything clustering near 0.5 — Seismo only sorts and thresholds, so this is safe. Top/Mismatch views in Magnitu still show the raw model composite.

Measuring Quality

Magnitu holds back ~10–20% of your labels as a stable test set (the same entries stay in test across retrains, keyed by entry id). It calculates Accuracy and an F1 Score, which is crucial because it penalizes models that inflate accuracy by simply calling everything "noise".

Known Biases & Limitations

Text Length Recipe weights are length-normalized for PHP. Embeddings use chunk pooling (~1800 chars per E5 window, up to 4–6 chunks) so long lex/Leg bodies are not truncated to a single 512-token slice.
Source Bias The recipe includes source weights (e.g. RSS toward noise). The deep model ignores source types unless they use distinctive language.
Class Imbalance Rare classes (like leads) use balanced class weights during training. Reasoning and time-decay knobs adjust per-label sample_weight instead of duplicating rows.
Recency Bias Unless Label Time-Decay is enabled, all labels hold equal weight regardless of age.

V. Configuration

Settings are divided into global (shared) and per-profile knobs.

Profile Settings (Saved Per Profile)

Minimum Labels to Train Default 20. The safety gate before a model can generalize.
Recipe Top Keywords Default 200. Number of words/phrases pushed to the Seismo recipe.
Alert Threshold Default 0.75. The score above which Seismo highlights an entry as an alert.
Lead Discovery Blend Default 0, Max 0.25. Mixes extra investigation_lead probability into the final relevance score.
Label Time-Decay Default Off. Applies a Label-Aware split-speed formula: ephemeral daily clutter (Noise rows) decays rapidly to match current news trends, while high-value historical alerts (Investigation Leads) are algorithmically protected from decay to maintain permanent training relevance.
Recipe Optimization Optional grid search over export caps (unigram / phrase / source ceilings) to maximize correlation between the local model and the PHP-parity recipe scorer on labeled holdout entries. Legal-template floors apply inside the search.
Reasoning-Weight Boost Default Off. Assigns a heavier training multiplier to labels with reasoning.
Synthetic Label Weight Default 0.5. Confirmed Gemini labels count at this fraction of human label weight during training and distillation (1.0 = off).

Global Settings (Shared Across All Profiles)

Legal-Signal Patterns: A user-configured list of regex/phrases. Matches are scanned on the full entry body, surfaced in natural-language context and extractive snippets in the embedded text, and boosted in the Seismo recipe. Changing this list invalidates all cached embeddings globally.
