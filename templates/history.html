I. The Dawn of Magnitu: Version 1.x (The Prototype Era)

Validating the premise that a system can learn an analyst's editorial priorities from feedback alone.

The project began as an experimental prototype designed to solve information overload for professional monitoring desks. In this initial stage, the platform focused on validating the basic premise: can a system learn an individual analyst's editorial priorities based entirely on implicit and explicit feedback?

Key Technical & Operational Milestones

Foundational Scoring Scale Established the core 0 to 100 relevance scale to rank incoming entries, shifting the workflow from a basic chronological feed to a priority-sorted stream.
Four-Label Taxonomy Defined the rigid four-class system: Investigation Lead (highest action value), Important (core beat material), Background (contextual information), and Noise (clutter, press releases, duplicates).
Relevance Feedback Loop Introduced a simple interface where an analyst could click through documents, tag them, and immediately build a local classification profile.
💡 What This Means for Dummies

In the prototype era, Magnitu was essentially a smart, digital sorting tray. Instead of forcing you to look at an endless, overwhelming wall of news sorted blindly by time, the system watched what you manually clicked on. If you kept flagging corporate financial filings as important and ignoring generic PR wires, it tried its best to push corporate text to the top of your feed.

Concrete Example: Imagine an analyst covering corporate malfeasance. An article arrives titled "Federal Prosecutor Opens Probe into Swiss Bank Entity." In v1.x, the analyst manually tags this as an Investigation Lead. The system logs this and looks for direct keyword matches in future articles. If another article arrives containing the words "Federal Prosecutor" or "Probe," its score gets bumped up slightly on the 0–100 dashboard slider.

II. The Cognitive Leap: Version 2.x (The Deep Learning & Distillation Bridge)

Deep transformers and the first keyword recipe bridge to PHP scoring.

As data volume grew, simple keyword matching and basic statistical text filters struggled with linguistic nuances, metaphors, and cross-lingual alerts (e.g., matching a Swiss impact within a German or French document). Version 2.x completely overhauled the system's core intelligence, introducing deep learning language processing.

Key Technical & Operational Milestones

Deep Transformers Replaced basic text filters with a heavy multilingual transformer (xlm-roberta-base). Terms like Untersuchung and investigation could land near each other in a shared vector space.
The Distillation Bridge Because production Seismo runs on high-speed PHP, it could not natively run heavy Python models. Version 2.x introduced Knowledge Distillation: a TF-IDF student mimics the transformer's teacher predictions.
Keyword Recipe Export The student's coefficients compile into a lightweight JSON file of weighted keywords (unigrams, bigrams, trigrams). The recipe is pushed to PHP for sub-millisecond live scoring.
💡 What This Means for Dummies

Magnitu upgraded from a basic keyword finder to a system that understands the meaning of sentences across multiple languages. Because this "big AI brain" was too heavy and slow to run every single millisecond a page loaded on your website, the system had the big brain act as a teacher. It read the data, figured out the deep patterns, and wrote a simplified, highly effective "cheat sheet" (the keyword recipe) for your web server to use instantly.

Concrete Example: A French EUR-Lex notice discusses accès au marché des pays tiers without mentioning Switzerland. You previously labeled similar English and German trade-impact text as Important. The transformer groups the concepts; the distilled recipe learns phrases like "third country" and "market access" so Seismo's PHP scorer still flags the French item high on your dashboard—even though the exact Swiss keyword never appears.

III. The Modern Automation & Multi-Profile Grid: Version 3.0 to 3.5 (The Satellite Era)

Distributed newsrooms, shared embeddings, and refined training for production desks.

While Version 2.x established powerful modeling capabilities, it operated as a monolithic single-user tool that struggled with cold-start data problems and required manual tuning. The Version 3.x series—culminating in Version 3.5—was built to support distributed newsrooms with shared data pools but diverging beats.

This era refined the system's core machine learning pipeline to resolve subtle logical blind spots and training inefficiencies.

Key Technical & Operational Milestones

Multi-Profile & Satellites A mothership-and-satellite paradigm: embeddings run once globally; each profile trains on its own labels and pushes scores to independent Satellite PHP installs.
Gemini Synthetic Scorer Optional batch-prompted Gemini pipeline for synthetic labels and reasoning notes, accelerating convergence before manual review.
Multilingual E5 Shift Upgraded to multilingual-e5-base, with passage: prefixing at embed time as required by the E5 family.
Natural Language Metadata Publisher and feed context is wrapped in fluent prose sentences instead of raw database notation (source_type=…), aligning metadata with the model's text pre-training.
Multi-Layer Perceptron Head Each profile trains a non-linear MLP Neural Classifier on frozen embeddings—capable of mapping complex geometric concept borders.
Out-of-Fold Calibration Temperature scaling fit on out-of-fold training predictions; when classes are too sparse for OOF, calibration safely defaults to T=1.0.
Soft-Label Distillation The TF-IDF student trains on the teacher's full probability vectors (not just argmax labels) via a continuous multi-output regression matrix, preserving nuance on borderline entries.
Recipe Cap Optimization During distillation, a bounded minimization sweep (L-BFGS-B) over unigram, phrase, and source export caps programmatically picks parameters that best track the full model.
Label-Aware Time Decay Optional sample-weight decay rules: transient clutter (noise) fades quickly, while vital priority alerts (investigation_lead) are algorithmically shielded against decay over time.
💡 What This Means for Dummies

Version 3.x is built for a newsroom where everyone reads the same firehose but cares about different things. The heavy reading (embeddings) happens once. Your tax desk and your trade desk each get their own "brain" and their own Seismo dashboard, without duplicating the entire ingest pipeline.

Concrete Example: The mothership ingests an intricate 60-page cross-border legislative framework issued by the EU Council regarding third-country financial market access. The Tax Desk profile—trained heavily on corporate financial audits—automatically grades it as low-priority, background administrative text. Simultaneously, the Trade Desk profile evaluates the exact same entry. Its non-linear MLP head detects a complex geometric intersection between cross-border compliance exclusions and third-country regimes, immediately flagging it as a critical Investigation Lead with an uninflated, calibrated confidence score of 89%. The soft-label distillation system maps this nuance into an optimized keyword recipe, alerting the Trade Desk's satellite platform in milliseconds while the Tax satellite stays perfectly quiet. Even if this document is referenced two years from now, label-aware time decay guarantees this vital precedent retains full training weight and is never buried by modern noise cycles.
